Where: the chip on the left shows the scope, the open folder and its subfolders (or Starred). Matches in other folders are listed below a divider. Click the chip, or press Backspace in the empty box, to switch to all mail; Shift+Enter searches all mail at once.

Only capital AND, OR, NOT are operators. Words are matched in the text, subject and addresses. Searches that combine words with AND or NOT check up to the 500 newest matches of each word; the status line says when there were more.

    Actions apply to the selected messages, or to the highlighted one if none are selected. Right-click a message for the same actions.

    Right-click a folder and choose Show attachments to list every attachment of its mail in place of its messages; click an attachment to see its message, or its type badge to open the attachment as well, and press Esc to go back to the messages. Tick Subfolders to include the attachments of its subfolders; the folder keeps that choice on this computer.

    Contacts, at the bottom of the folder list, lists every address in your mail, Spam and Trash aside, in place of the messages, each under one of three tabs: People, whom you have written to or pinned; Others, whom you have not written to; and Hidden, the addresses that are never suggested, where automated senders start. Each is listed latest first, People after the ones you pinned. Select a person to write to them, see all mail with them, pin them to the top of People, or hide them; the pin and the eye on a row do the same for that row. A pinned address cannot be hidden, nor a hidden one pinned. The trash bin on a row (or Del) deletes a contact: it stays out whatever mail names it later, until a rebuild. A pinned contact cannot be deleted. Contacts stays as you left it while you look at mail and come back. It mirrors the mail you keep: an address is forgotten once no mail with it is left outside Spam and Trash, unless you pinned, hid, named or added it, or mail was copied to it (Cc or Bcc). Rename (F2) changes the name a contact is shown under. Right-click Contacts to export them to a text file you can edit, or to import one; Settings can rebuild them from your mail, keeping your pins and the names you gave. Select several to write to all of them at once. A person's mail opened from Contacts goes back to Contacts with Esc.
