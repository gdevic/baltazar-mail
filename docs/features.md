# Features

What Baltazar Mail does, in full. The two editions, for Yahoo Mail™ and for Gmail™, share all of it; the last section says what differs in the Gmail edition. Everything works with the mouse; press `?` in the app for the keyboard shortcuts.

- **Reading:** three-column or stacked layout with resizable panes, sixteen palettes, eight light and eight dark in a range of hues and mostly in light and dark pairs (two of them plain black and white), a contrast slider, one text size setting that scales the whole interface and the message, a zoom for the message pane alone, read or written, shown for a moment over the pane (+, - and 0, Ctrl+= / Ctrl+- / Ctrl+0 in the composer, or Ctrl+wheel over it), a row height setting, a Starred view across all folders but Spam and Trash, and remote images loaded automatically except in Spam and Trash. In a dark theme messages are darkened to match, keeping their colours' hues and leaving images alone; the ☀ button in the message header shows them in their original colours instead.
- **Links:** Settings › Links in messages can remove tracking from the links in mail (off by default). Tags such as `utm_source`, `fbclid`, `mc_eid` and `_hsenc` are dropped, and a redirect that carries its destination (Google™, YouTube, Facebook, Instagram, Outlook SafeLinks, Substack) is replaced by that destination, also cleaned. It applies to the reader, printing, HTML attachments and the quote in a reply or forward, so a forwarded newsletter does not hand on your subscriber id; links you paste into your own message are left as they are. It is done without any request, so a link through a mailing service's own click tracker (Mailchimp, SendGrid and most others), whose destination is not in the link, stays as it is: finding out where it goes would mean visiting it, which is the click it records. Changing the setting redraws the open message, so a link the cleaning broke can be had as the sender wrote it.
- **Tracking pictures:** the invisible pictures that tell a sender you opened a message are taken out of every message
- **Mailbox actions:** read/unread, star, archive, move, delete, spam and not spam, on one message or a selection (click, Shift-click for a range, Ctrl-click for single rows), with multi-level undo and redo. A trash icon beside each row's date deletes it without opening it. The reader has its own Delete button, for the open message alone; for a message in Trash it reads Undelete and moves it to Inbox without asking. Right-click a message for these and for Reply, Reply all, Forward and Find all from, with each shortcut shown.
- **Undo:** `z` undoes the last action and Shift+Z redoes it, back through the last 50 actions until the page is reloaded or closed: moving, archiving, deleting, spam and not spam, read and unread, stars, and in Contacts pinning, hiding, renaming, deleting, forgetting and importing. Undo and Redo at the right of the status bar do the same in every view, Contacts included, and their tooltips say what each would take; ▾ beside them, or `y`, opens the undo history above them, twelve entries tall and scrolling for the rest. It names each action, what it did to which messages or contacts and when (`delete "Budget report" from Alex`, `archive 3 messages: "Budget report", "Lunch?", ...`), newest first, each with Undo; an action undone stays in its place, struck through, with Redo (↓ and ↑ and Enter, or a click). Any of them can be taken back or done again alone, out of order, unless a later action changed the same thing since (a message's folder, its read flag or its star, or a contact as a whole): its Undo or Redo is greyed out then, and its tooltip names the one to take first. Messages waiting to be sent are listed on top, each with Cancel. A new action of yours clears what could be redone. Contacts forgetting an address with no mail left, which it does by itself, does not, but for an undone change to that same address.
- **Folders:** right-click a folder to show its attachments (first, since it only looks), mark it all read, create a subfolder, rename, move or delete it (only when it and its subfolders are empty); right-click Trash or Spam to empty it. What cannot apply is greyed out, with the reason as its tooltip: Mark all read when nothing is unread, Delete while the folder or a subfolder holds mail, Empty when there is nothing to empty. The Folders heading above your own folders creates top-level folders and opens or closes the first level of subfolders; a double click opens or closes one folder. Subfolders of Inbox are shown under Inbox, as Yahoo™ files them.
- **Search:** scoped to the open folder and its subfolders, with matches in other folders (Spam and Trash aside) listed below a divider. Words must all match; `AND`, `OR`, `NOT` (or a leading `-`), parentheses, quoted phrases and the fields `from:` `to:` `subject:` `has:attachment` `is:starred` `is:read` `is:unread` `after:` `before:` refine it. The `?` in the search box explains the syntax.
- **Writing:** new message, reply, reply all and forward (with the original's attachments), drafts saved automatically, rich-text or plain-text editing with fonts, sizes and colours, pasted or dropped inline images, attachments, and undo-send. Click a picture in the message to size it (Small, Medium, Large, Original, or drag its corner) or delete it (Ctrl+Z brings it back). A picture made smaller is sent whole, as Gmail and Outlook do, so it can be opened at full size; Settings › Writing can send it at the size shown instead, for a smaller message (twice the shown width, so it stays sharp on high-density screens; GIFs go whole to keep their animation, and drafts always keep the whole picture).
- **Contacts:** every address in your own mail, learned from the message headers: those of every folder but Spam, Trash and Drafts when it is first built (a few minutes for a large mailbox, while you use the app), and of the new mail in Inbox and Sent from then on. Contacts, at the bottom of the folder list (or `g c`), lists them in place of the messages, each under one of three tabs: **People**, whom you have written to or pinned, most recent first; **Others**, whom you have not written to; and **Hidden**, the addresses that are never suggested, where automated senders start. Type to filter by name or address: each tab counts its matches, and an empty one says where they are. Select a person and the reading pane becomes their page: Write, All mail, Mail from, Mail to, Copy address, Pin and Hide, other addresses with the same name, and the ten most recent messages with them, each opening in the person's mail; Ctrl+wheel over those messages (or + and -) zooms them, at a size of their own. A mail button is greyed out, with the reason, once it is known there is no such mail. The pin on a row pins it to the top of People, and the eye hides it or, under Hidden, unhides it; a pinned address cannot be hidden, nor a hidden one pinned, and `z` undoes a hide or a pin. The pin or the eye of a selected row acts on everyone selected. The trash bin that floats before a row's date, as on a message row, deletes a contact under any tab (so do Delete on its page, and Del): it leaves every list and the suggestions, and stays out whatever mail names it later, until a rebuild. `z` right after takes a deletion back, and so does importing a file that lists the contact. A pinned contact has no bin and cannot be deleted. Select several people (Ctrl-click, Shift-click, `x`, or Ctrl+A for everyone listed) to write one message to all of them, find mail from or to any of them, copy their addresses, or hide or pin them together; right-click a row for the same actions. Enter shows all mail with the person, and Esc there comes back to Contacts, which is always shown again as you left it: the same tab, filter, person and place in the list. Rename (or F2) changes the name a contact is shown under: type it in place of the one on the contact's page, and it is shown before any name the mail gives. An empty name goes back to the one from the mail, and `z` undoes a rename. The address cannot be edited, since the address is what a contact is. Right-click Contacts in the folder list to export every contact to a text file, a contact a line as mail writes an address (`Name <address>`, with `pinned` or `hidden` after it), which can be edited by hand, and to import one: Import and merge takes the names, pins and hides the file says and adds its new addresses, leaving everything else; Import and replace makes the list what the file says, removing every contact that is not in it, so it asks first and refuses a file with a line it cannot read. Each import is one undo. Settings › Contacts says how many contacts there are, and Rebuild there reads the headers of all your mail again and builds Contacts anew, keeping only your pins and the names you gave: what you hid, deleted or added goes, and the mail decides again. Contacts mirrors the mail you keep: an address is forgotten once no mail with it is left outside Spam and Trash (when you delete that mail or mark it as spam here, or when you open the person and none is found), unless you pinned, hid, named or added it, or mail was copied to it (Cc or Bcc); undo brings it back. Whom you have written to is told by who wrote a message, not by its folder: a reply of yours kept in Inbox counts, and a person whose page shows a message you wrote is listed under People from then on. While Contacts is first being built, it says so above the list and in the status bar, with how many message headers are read (only headers are read, never a message) and, at Yahoo, how long is left, and what it has learned so far can be used. An update that changes what Contacts records builds it anew once, keeping everything you chose (what you hid, pinned, named, added and deleted); otherwise the book is kept as built.
- **Address suggestions:** while writing, and in the search box: the address typed first, then the ones that complete what was typed, then those that merely contain it, each ranked by how recently you wrote to or heard from the person. While writing, Enter, Tab or a comma takes the first suggestion only when it completes what was typed; once a whole address is typed, they keep that address, and Ctrl+Enter always sends what was typed. A name typed in the search box offers the people it could mean: picking one shows all mail with them, or completes the address after `from:` or `to:`, while Enter alone still searches for what was typed. Automated senders you never wrote to (noreply, notifications, bulk-mail domains) start out hidden, so they are not suggested until you unhide them in Contacts, and × beside a suggestion while writing hides that address.
- **Attachments:** listed under the message and previewed in place: PDF with selectable text and zoom, Word, Excel and other spreadsheets, PowerPoint, older Word, RTF and OpenDocument text and slides through Yahoo's converter, images, text and code, CSV, HTML, audio, video and ZIP listings. Anything else up to 100 KB can be read as text, or as a hex dump when it is binary. A picture opens whole on a grey checkerboard, as in an image editor, with its size and zoom in the title bar: the mouse wheel zooms it about the pointer (+, - and 0 work too), stopping at its own size on the way, and while it is larger than the pane, dragging moves it. In a dark theme the viewers take the app's colours: text, code, hex dumps, CSV, spreadsheets and ZIP listings are drawn in the palette, the pages of a PDF, a Word document or a converted one stay white on its background, and an HTML attachment is darkened as a message is (☀ in the message header shows both in their original colours). Right-click the attachments to download them all in one ZIP file, or Ctrl-click some of them first (a plain click still previews) and choose Download selected. A ZIP file holds at most 100 files and 100 MB by the sizes listed, and more is refused with the advice to select fewer. Its files are stored as they are, uncompressed and side by side with no folders, each dated with its message, and a name that repeats gets " (2)", " (3)" and so on. The files are fetched one message at a time while the status bar counts them, and Esc stops it; one that cannot be fetched is left out and named in a notice. A single file is saved as itself.
- **Attachments of a folder:** right-click a folder › Show attachments lists every file attached to its mail in place of its messages, newest first, with its type, name, sender (the recipient for your own sent mail), size and date. An attachment sent again is listed once (on Yahoo when Yahoo names its content the same, on Gmail by the same name and size), and pictures built into a message's text or a sender's nameless logos are left out. The list is built from the search results alone, 100 messages at a time (Load more, or n), so no message is opened to build it. Click an attachment to see its message in the reader, which has no Delete button here, since deleting the message would take its attachments with it; a click on its type badge (PDF, IMG and so on, outlined under the mouse), Enter or a double click also opens the attachment, as a badge under a message does, and the viewer's arrows step through the whole list; right-click for Preview, Download, Download all, Download selected and Show the message in its folder. Ctrl-click, Shift-click, x and Ctrl+A select attachments as they select messages in a folder, and the two ZIP downloads work as under a message, named after the folder; all is every attachment listed so far. A folder with subfolders has a Subfolders box that adds their attachments, each named with its subfolder; the folder keeps that choice on this computer. Nothing is added to the folder tree: the list stays until you open a folder, search or press Esc, which goes back to the folder's messages.
- **Also:** a red dot on the tab icon and toolbar button when new mail arrives while the tab is in the background, or arrived while the browser was closed and the tab opens in the background (handy for a pinned tab), contact cards (right-click a message › About sender..., or hover an address in the message header) with the person's recent messages, each opening in its folder, and a link to the person in Contacts, printing, anything that failed shown in a bar under the top bar (with details to copy for a bug report), and all settings kept between sessions.

## Gmail: what differs

The Gmail edition has the same interface and the same features, with these differences that come from Gmail itself: Gmail's labels are the folders (a message with no label is in All Mail, and Archive moves it there); a move takes the message out of the folder it is shown in and keeps its other labels, also in Trash and Spam, and undo puts back exactly what the move changed; Spam and Trash are Gmail's; Mark all read, Empty Trash and Empty Spam work on the label, 100,000 messages at a time; renaming a folder renames the labels nested under it. There is no document converter, so PowerPoint, Keynote, older Word, RTF and OpenDocument text and slides offer Download (and, up to 100 KB, a text or hex view) instead of a preview (PDF, Word .docx, spreadsheets of every kind, images, text and ZIP preview as before). Images in messages load straight from the senders' servers when the setting allows, since Gmail's API hands over the message as the sender wrote it (see [PRIVACY.md](../PRIVACY.md)). Gmail's API has a quota per user (by default 6,000 units a minute, where a list of 100 messages costs about 500), so the extension paces its requests and builds Contacts over time: starting two minutes after the mailbox first opens, it reads the newest 2,000 messages of each of Sent, Inbox and All Mail a little at a time, only once you have left the app alone for a minute (or its tab is in the background), which adds up to an hour at most and carries on across sessions; until it is done, address suggestions come from what has been read so far. How the adapter maps Gmail onto the client is in `gmail-api.md`.
